Beyond Immigration

This map is a smaller version of my final project, which looks at how ICE detention and deportation affect UCLA Asian American students whose loved ones were taken away. Instead of only focusing on the people who were detained or deported, I wanted to focus on the people around them and how that loss can affect school, family life, finances, and mental health.

Background

In 2025, after Donald Trump returned to office, immigration policies expanded ICE enforcement across workplaces, businesses, neighborhoods, and other public spaces. Although these actions were framed as immigration enforcement, the effects did not stop with the individuals who were detained or deported.

A lot of public discussion focuses on Latinx communities because they have been disproportionately targeted, but that can narrow public understanding of how detention and deportation also affect other immigrant communities. This project focuses on UCLA Asian American students to show that immigration enforcement can shape emotional well-being, financial stability, education, and everyday life for the loved ones left behind.

Map Focus

Each point stands in for an anonymous response and highlights one kind of impact left behind after detention or deportation.
